# Phase 0 gap read — what a datacenter vantage is worth (RU, 2026-07-28 .. 2026-08-27)

**Question.** DESIGN.md Correction 3 asserts that a cheap RU VPS may sit behind a *different* TSPU
box, or none — making a datacenter vantage potentially **categorically wrong** about the consumer
path. Everything downstream leans on that: it is why client-as-sensor is the unlock rather than the
capstone, and why a VPS-only Phase 1 must scope its claim to the DC path. Until now it was an
argument. This is the first measurement.

**Method.** OONI's existing Russian coverage for circumvention-tool reachability, split by
reporting ASN into consumer operators and hosting/DC providers, block rate compared. Recruitment-
free: no volunteers, no new vantages, nobody put at risk. Full numbers in
[`gap-2026-08-27.md`](gap-2026-08-27.md) / `.json`, reproducible with
`python phase0/ooni_gap.py --since 2026-07-28 --until 2026-08-27`.

Block rate is `anomaly / (anomaly + ok)`; OONI failures are excluded from the denominator. ASNs are
bucketed on **identity evidence only** — holder name, website, what the company sells — never on
measured rate, and the ambiguous ones are left unclassified and reported with their own rate. See
`asn_classes.json`.

---

## 1. The finding: there is no single DC-vs-consumer gap. It is provider-specific.

The pooled comparison is the least interesting cut of this data. Within the hosting bucket, in the
same country, in the same month, on the same test:

| test | most-blocked DC provider | least-blocked DC provider | spread |
|---|---|---|---|
| `tor` | Cloud.ru 100.0% (n=28) | Beget 13.0% (n=23) | **87 pp** |
| `psiphon` | MTW 86.7% (n=30) | Beget 0.0% (n=25) | **87 pp** |
| `telegram` | IHOR 100.0% (n=44) | Beget 4.0% (n=25) | **96 pp** |

Consumer operators do not spread like this — they cluster (`tor` 99.3%, `telegram` 85.8%,
`psiphon` 74.8%, each with CIs a fraction of a point wide across 30k–50k measurements).

**Beget (AS198610) is near-clean on all three tests** while consumer networks are 75–99% blocked.
That is a concrete instance of Correction 3's "a different box, or none" — not a lenient version of
the consumer measurement, a different measurement.

## 2. The gap's sign is not stable across tests

| test | consumer | hosting/DC | gap | robust? |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| `psiphon` | 74.8% (n=49,430) | 9.8% (n=1,097) | **+65.0 pp** | yes — +50.1 pp dropping the largest DC ASN, +70.7 pp dropping the second |
| `tor` | 99.3% (n=29,963) | 92.6% (n=1,047) | **+6.7 pp** | weakly — see §3 |
| `telegram` | 85.8% (n=30,148) | 93.7% (n=1,034) | **−7.9 pp** | reverses — DC *more* blocked; holds at −4.6/−7.2 pp under the same sensitivity |

So "datacenter paths are more lenient" is false as a general statement. It is strongly true for
Psiphon, marginal for Tor, and **backwards** for Telegram. Any claim of the form "the DC path shows
X, so consumers see roughly X" is unsupported in either direction.

## 3. Where this read is weak, stated plainly

- **The Tor gap is confounded.** The unclassified tail (93 ASNs, n=12,403) sits at 92.4% — statist-
  ically indistinguishable from the DC bucket's 92.6%, and well below the consumer bucket's 99.3%.
  The tail is mostly small regional ISPs, so for `tor` the split may be tracking operator size
  rather than DC-vs-consumer. For `psiphon` the tail (74.2%) lands right on the consumer bucket
  (74.8%), which is what a clean split should look like — that gap is not an artifact of
  classification.
- **The DC bucket is small and self-selected.** 5–6 ASNs, n≈1,000 per test, dominated by two
  providers. These are people who chose to run ooniprobe on a VPS; some may be VPN exits or
  proxies rather than plain hosts.
- **Wrong instrument for the real question.** These are tool/endpoint reachability tests. Lokhotron
  measures VPN-protocol handshake shaping. This is directional evidence about the *vantage*, not a
  prediction of what the battery will see.
- **No DC-side coverage at all for `torsf`, and none classified for `riseupvpn`** (318 graded
  measurements, all in unclassified ASNs).
- A gap **supports** Correction 3; its absence would not refute it. These are two populations
  measured by the same test, not one measurement from two vantages. Only `--dc-measurements` with a
  real RU VPS closes that.

## 4. What this changes for Phase 1

1. **Provider choice is a measurement decision, not a procurement decision.** Renting the wrong RU
   VPS can produce a sensor that sees almost nothing (Beget-like) or one that sees near-consumer
   blocking (MTW-like). Picking on price alone is picking your findings at random.
2. **One RU VPS cannot be called representative.** The honest Phase 1 claim was already scoped to
   "the DC path"; this narrows it further, to *this provider's* DC path. Two or three RU VPSes at
   **different providers** is a materially better instrument than one, and still inside the ~$10/mo
   envelope. Worth doing at provisioning time rather than discovering later.
3. **Keep the OONI comparison in the write-up permanently.** It is the only consumer-side reference
   available before Phase 3, and it is what stops a DC finding from being over-read.
4. **Re-run this before provisioning.** It is one command and the picture is month-specific; a
   provider that looks clean today may be filtered by the time you rent.

## 5. Status of the open questions this closes

- *"TSPU's current DC-vs-consumer treatment"* — **partially answered.** The gap is real but
  provider-specific and test-dependent, not a constant. Correction 3 stands, and is stronger than
  when stated: the risk isn't that a VPS is a lenient sensor, it's that it may be an unrelated one.
- *"OONI current API shapes / which RU tests are populated"* — **answered.** `api.ooni.io`
  `/api/v1/aggregation` with `axis_x=probe_asn` is live and well-populated for RU: `psiphon` 79k,
  `signal` 68k, `whatsapp` 47k, `telegram` 45k, `tor` 45k, `torsf` 1.2k, `riseupvpn` 322 (30-day
  window). `vanilla_tor` is excluded — ~95% of its RU measurements are *failures*, so its rate
  describes the test, not the path. ASN names come from RIPEstat `as-names` (bulk, one call).
  CAIDA's AS-classification dataset was checked as a classification source and its public path is
  404 — hence name-based classification with published rules.
